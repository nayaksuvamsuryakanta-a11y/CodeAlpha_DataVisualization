### Title: Netflix Data Visualization — CodeAlpha Internship Task 3

**[0:00 – 0:20] INTRO**
"Hi, I'm Suvam Suryakanta Nayak, and in this video I'm walking you through Task 3 of my CodeAlpha Data Analytics Internship — a complete data visualization project on the Netflix Movies and TV Shows dataset.

I'll show you the dataset, the five business questions I set out to answer, the charts I built, the dashboard, and the key insights."

---

**[0:20 – 0:45] DATASET**
"The dataset is the Netflix Movies and TV Shows dataset from Kaggle — around 8,800 titles with information on type, country, release year, content rating, genre, and duration.

I loaded it with pandas, parsed dates, split duration into minutes for movies and seasons for TV shows, and extracted primary genre and country from the comma-separated fields."

---

**[0:45 – 1:15] BUSINESS QUESTIONS**
"Before writing a single line of charting code, I defined five business questions:
1. What's the split between Movies and TV Shows?
2. How has the library grown over time — and is there seasonality?
3. Which countries and genres dominate?
4. How long are Netflix movies, and does rating affect that?
5. How mature is the content, and how many seasons do TV shows run?

Every chart in the project answers one of these questions."

---

**[1:15 – 2:00] CHART DEMOS**
"Here's a quick tour of the charts:

The bar chart confirms movies make up about 70% of the catalog.

The line chart shows explosive growth from 2015 to 2019, followed by a plateau.

The scatter plot with a trendline shows Netflix increasingly adds recently-released content — the median lag between a title's release and its Netflix addition is only about one or two years.

The violin chart reveals that R-rated and TV-MA movies run longer than children's content — with tighter distributions for family titles.

The grouped bar chart shows International Movies and Dramas dominate both content types.

And the correlation heatmap confirms release year and year-added are the most tightly correlated numeric features."

---

**[2:00 – 2:25] DASHBOARD**
"All of this comes together in the summary dashboard — KPI tiles at the top for total titles, movies, TV shows, and countries, with four panels below covering the type split, yearly trend, top countries, and genre and rating distributions.

This is saved as a 300 dpi PNG and is the single shareable summary of the whole project."

---

**[2:25 – 2:50] INSIGHTS & CLOSING**
"Key takeaways: Netflix skews adult — TV-MA is the top rating. Over 70% of TV shows have just one season. The US and India lead content production. And the peak growth years are behind us, signalling a shift from volume to curation.

The full notebook, Python script, and all visuals are on GitHub — link in the post caption.
